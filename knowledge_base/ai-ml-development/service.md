Service overview
Osiyatech's AI & Machine Learning service helps businesses turn their data into actionable intelligence. The
tagline is "Build the Future with AI, Machine Learning & Generative AI." The service designs, builds and
deploys custom machine learning models, predictive analytics, natural language processing (NLP), computer
vision, document intelligence and generative AI applications, including chatbots powered by large language
models (LLMs).
The service covers two kinds of AI. Traditional machine learning finds patterns in data, makes predictions
and analyses information; it is used for forecasting, classification and image recognition. Generative AI creates
new content such as text, summaries and conversational answers, using LLMs such as those from OpenAI.
Osiyatech builds both and often combines them in one business application.
Osiyatech focuses on practical, production-ready AI rather than experiments. Projects start with a data and
opportunity assessment, move through model development and validation, and end with AI integrated into the
client's existing websites, mobile apps, enterprise software or business platforms through APIs, usually without
rebuilding those systems. After deployment, performance is monitored and optimised as business needs
change.
Business problems solved
•
Data that isn't being used: businesses collect large amounts of data but struggle to get insight from it.
Predictive analytics and ML models turn it into forecasts, trends and recommendations.
•
Generic AI answers: off-the-shelf chatbots don't know a company's products, policies or documents.
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) grounds AI answers in the company's own content, so responses
are more accurate and relevant.
•
Sensitive data and privacy concerns: some organisations can't send confidential data to public AI
services. LLM fine-tuning and private deployment offer secure hosting and controlled access.
•
Repetitive manual work: document processing, information lookup and routine customer questions
consume staff time. NLP, document intelligence and conversational AI automate this work.
•
Visual inspection done by hand: computer vision automates image-based tasks, for example measuring
freight dimensions in logistics.
•
Uncertainty about where AI fits: many businesses don't know where AI would help. The discovery and
data strategy stage assesses data readiness and identifies realistic AI opportunities.

Features and capabilities
AI Chatbots & Conversational AI
Intelligent, context-aware chatbots powered by LLMs and business knowledge, delivering accurate, relevant
conversations across digital platforms. Typical uses include customer support, website assistants, internal
helpdesks and sales enquiry handling.
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
Knowledge-grounded AI that retrieves relevant information from documents, databases and enterprise content
before generating an answer. RAG makes AI responses more reliable because they are based on the
company's own up-to-date information, not only the model's general training. Typical uses include document
Q&A, policy and knowledge-base assistants, and internal search.
LLM Fine-Tuning & Private Deployment
Customising large language models with domain-specific data and deploying them with secure hosting options
and controlled access to sensitive information. Fine-tuning adapts a model's behaviour, tone or domain
expertise. Private deployment keeps data within controlled infrastructure.
Other AI and ML capabilities
•
Custom AI and ML development: models built for a specific business problem.
•
Predictive analytics and forecasting: predicting demand, sales, risk or other outcomes from historical
data.
•
Generative AI solutions: content generation, summarisation and AI-assisted workflows.
•
Natural language processing (NLP): understanding, classifying and extracting meaning from text.
•
Computer vision and image recognition: analysing images and video to detect, measure or classify
objects.
•
Document intelligence: extracting and structuring information from documents.
•
AI model integration: connecting AI models to existing applications through APIs.
•
LLM-powered applications: software built around large language model capabilities.
Object Detection & Computer Vision
Models that find, classify and read objects in images and video, using modern detection architectures such as
YOLOv8. Typical uses include automated meter reading, product and defect detection, counting and measuring
objects, and monitoring from camera feeds. Detection models can run in the cloud or, where speed and
connectivity matter, directly on mobile devices.
Computer Vision for AR & VR
Computer vision that powers augmented and virtual reality experiences: recognising surfaces, objects and
environments so digital content can be placed accurately in the real world. These capabilities are built together
with the Mobile App Development and UI/UX Design teams.
Technologies used
•
LLMs and generative AI (language models and the frameworks that orchestrate them): OpenAI,
Hugging Face, LangChain, LlamaIndex.
•
Machine learning and deep learning (training and running predictive and vision models): PyTorch,
TensorFlow, Scikit-learn, Python.
•
Computer vision and object detection: YOLOv8 for real-time object detection, plus PyTorch and
TensorFlow vision models; TensorFlow Lite for running models on mobile devices.
•
Vector databases and data infrastructure (storing embeddings for semantic search and RAG):
Pinecone, PostgreSQL, pgvector.
•
Redis (fast AI applications): used to cache LLM responses and embeddings to cut cost and response time,
store chatbot conversation history and session state, and manage queues for background AI jobs such as
document processing.
•
Backend and deployment (serving AI in production): FastAPI, Docker, Kubernetes, Kafka, AWS.

Target customers and use cases
AI & Machine Learning serves organisations that want to use their data and knowledge more effectively:
answering questions automatically, predicting what comes next, reading documents and images, or adding
intelligent features to existing products.
By company size
•
Startups: product companies building AI-first products or adding AI features, such as chatbots,
recommendations or summarisation, to stand out in their market. A proof of concept, often achievable in a
few weeks, helps them validate an AI idea with real users and investors before committing to a full build.
•
Small and medium businesses (SMEs): businesses that want practical AI without a data science team,
such as a website chatbot that answers customer questions from their own content, automated document
processing, or sales forecasting. Osiyatech handles everything from assessing their data to deploying and
maintaining the solution.
•
Enterprises: organisations with large data sets, document libraries and established systems, which need AI
integrated securely into existing platforms. They often need private LLM deployment with controlled access,
RAG over enterprise content, and computer vision or predictive models at scale.
By situation or need
•
Making company knowledge easy to access: organisations with manuals, policies, product information or
large document libraries that want an AI assistant, using RAG, to answer questions accurately from their
own content.
•
Improving customer support: businesses that want AI chatbots handling routine questions around the
clock.
•
Forecasting and planning: companies that want to predict demand, sales, risk or other outcomes from
historical data.
•
Automating visual inspection and reading: operations where people currently read meters, check images
or measure objects by hand, which computer vision and object detection can automate.
•
Processing documents at scale: teams that extract information from invoices, forms or contracts manually.
•
Protecting sensitive data: regulated or privacy-conscious organisations that need private AI deployment
instead of public AI services.
•
Adding AI to existing products: companies that want AI features in their current website, app or enterprise
software without rebuilding it.
•
Powering AR experiences: products that need computer vision to recognise real-world objects and
surfaces for AR features.
Industries and example use cases
•
EdTech and publishing: AI-assisted reading and learning experiences such as MentorBooth.
•
Utilities and energy: automated meter reading with object detection.
•
Manufacturing: defect detection, forecasting and process optimisation.
•
Logistics: image-based measurement and inspection automation.
•
Retail and e-commerce: product recommendations, AI shopping assistants and demand forecasting.
•
Financial services: document intelligence and predictive analytics.
•
Healthcare: document processing and knowledge assistants.
•
Legal and professional services: RAG assistants over large document collections.

Development process
1
Discovery and data strategy: analyse business objectives, data readiness and AI opportunities to define a
clear roadmap aligned with business goals.
2
AI model development and validation: select, develop, fine-tune and evaluate AI models for accuracy,
reliability and real-world performance.
3
Deployment and continuous optimisation: integrate AI into existing systems, then monitor and optimise
performance as business needs evolve.
A proof of concept is often built first to prove value before a full production build.
Requirements and dependencies
•
Customer inputs required: business data or knowledge sources (documents, databases, content) for
training, fine-tuning or RAG, depending on the approach. Data availability affects the timeline.
•
Technology or platform prerequisites: none required. Osiyatech recommends the most suitable
technology stack for each project. If the client already has a pre-selected technology stack, Osiyatech can
work with it.
•
Delivery timeline: a basic proof of concept may take a few weeks. A production-ready solution needs more
time for development, testing, deployment and optimisation.
•
Pricing: requires a project assessment.
