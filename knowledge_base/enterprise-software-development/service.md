Service overview
Osiyatech's Enterprise Software Development service builds custom software that supports complex business
operations. The tagline is "Enterprise Software Solutions, Built to Scale." The service designs and engineers
internal business platforms, enterprise portals, workflow systems and mission-critical applications that
streamline workflows, connect systems and support long-term growth.
Enterprise software is software that runs core organisational processes: internal operations, data management,
approvals, departmental workflows and the connections between them. Unlike off-the-shelf products,
Osiyatech's enterprise solutions are built around the client's own processes. That gives the business control
over features and integrations, and reduces its dependence on disconnected tools.
The service also covers legacy application modernization. Outdated systems can be upgraded, re-architected,
secured and moved to the cloud. Long-term maintenance and SLA-backed support keep business-critical
applications reliable after launch. The architecture is modular, API-first and microservice-based where
appropriate, so systems can grow with the organisation.
Business problems solved
•
Off-the-shelf software that doesn't fit: standard products force businesses to change how they work.
Custom enterprise software matches existing workflows and covers requirements standard software can't.
•
Disconnected tools and duplicated effort: departments work in separate systems and re-key data.
Enterprise applications and portals connect departments and centralise information.
•
Manual, repetitive processes: approvals, hand-offs and routine tasks slow operations down. Workflow
automation, approval systems and business rule engines remove manual steps.
•
Ageing legacy systems: outdated applications are hard to maintain, insecure and difficult to scale. Legacy
modernization upgrades technology, architecture and security, and moves suitable workloads to the cloud.
•
Systems that can't grow: monolithic applications struggle with more users and data. Microservices and
API-first architecture support growth.
•
Controlling who sees what: large organisations need fine-grained permissions. Role-based access control
and compliance features are built in.

Target customers and use cases
Enterprise Software Development serves organisations whose operations have become too complex for
off-the-shelf tools, or whose core systems are ageing, and who need dependable custom software built to last.
By company size
•
Startups and scale-ups: fast-growing companies whose early tools, such as spreadsheets and a
patchwork of SaaS apps, can no longer support their operations. They need an internal platform or portal
that scales with them, often built in phases so the most important modules come first.
•
Small and medium businesses (SMEs): established businesses with specific processes that standard
software can't support, who need custom applications to automate workflows, connect departments and give
staff, customers or partners self-service portals.
•
Enterprises: large organisations with mission-critical systems, many departments and complex integrations.
They need modular, API-first and microservice-based architectures, legacy modernization, role-based
access across large user bases, and long-term SLA-backed maintenance, often delivered by a Dedicated
Engineering Team.
By situation or need
•
Outgrowing off-the-shelf software: businesses forced into workarounds by products that don't match their
processes.
•
Connecting departments: organisations where teams work in separate systems and re-enter data.

Development process
1
Discovery: assess business workflows, technical requirements and existing infrastructure to set a clear
roadmap and scalable architecture.
2
Development: build secure, modular applications with modern technology, agile methods and clean coding
practices, for easy integration, flexibility and long-term maintainability.
3
Deployment: streamlined releases, thorough testing, performance optimisation and ongoing technical
support, with continuous improvement as requirements evolve.
Larger multi-module platforms are delivered in phases.
